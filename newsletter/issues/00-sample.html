Markets & Macro · this week

Inflation ran hot, and gold took the hit

US consumer prices, published on 14 October, came in warmer than forecast. The dollar and bond yields jumped, and gold dropped about 1.5% that day to roughly $4,100–4,150 an ounce. It is now down around 4% this year. The Fed meets on 27–28 October and markets mostly expect it to sit still.

Why gold hates this news: hot inflation means rates stay high for longer. Gold pays no interest, so when your cash and bonds pay you more, holding it looks less clever.

Why it matters
Inflation decides interest rates, and interest rates quietly decide almost everything else: mortgages, savings, stock prices and what your euros buy abroad.

Sources: World Gold Council

Art & Collecting · explained

Provenance: an object's paper trail

Provenance is the recorded history of who has owned a work, from the artist's studio until today. A solid provenance does two jobs: it helps prove the work is genuine, and it shows nobody has a claim to get it back.

Gaps make buyers nervous, especially for Europe between 1933 and 1945, when the Nazis looted huge amounts of art.

Why it matters
When a catalogue says "impeccable provenance," it means: we know exactly where this has been, and nobody will come knocking for it.
