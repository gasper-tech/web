01 · Spain · politics

Spain votes on 29 November, and housing is why

Pedro Sánchez called a snap general election on 5 October, three days after Congress threw out his two housing decrees (PP, Vox and Junts voted against). The government simply approved them again on 6 October, and the main one has been in force since 8 October. Congress's standing committee votes on them on 12 November, the day after a 24-hour general strike over housing and wages called by the CCOO and UGT unions. The campaign starts on 13 November.

The polls disagree. The state pollster CIS has the PSOE well ahead (34.0% to the PP's 22.2%), while the private polls put the PP first: the PolitPro average has PP 31.8%, PSOE 26.4%, Vox 18.9%. Every party is now campaigning on housing, so property rules could change twice in a few months: once by decree, and again under whoever wins.

What it means here
Buyers and investors on the coast tend to pause around elections. Expect some deals to wait for the result, and expect holiday-let rules (next story) to be a campaign topic.

02 · Holiday lets · tax

10% VAT on short holiday lets from 1 December

The housing decree in force since 8 October ends a long-standing exemption. From 1 December, renting a furnished home to the same guest for 30 nights or fewer will normally carry 10% VAT, unless it is the owner's main home. Until now these lets were VAT-free as long as no hotel-style services were offered. On a €2,000 week, that is €200 more for the guest or €200 less for the owner.

Councils in officially "stressed" housing markets can also add an IBI surcharge on tourist homes, of up to 150% for owners with four or more, but each council has to pass its own bylaw first. The same decree tightens seasonal lets (they must be justified, run over 31 days and generally no more than 12 months) and creates "Tu Casa" interest-free loans of up to €50,000, covering up to 20% of a first home's price. All of it stands or falls with the 12 November vote, and the government expects this decree to pass by one vote.

What it means here
If owners absorb the VAT, short-let income on the coast drops by roughly a tenth. Owners who already offer cleaning or breakfast were charging VAT anyway, so the change hits the simpler, self-managed lets hardest.

03 · Marbella plan · planning

Marbella finally has a new town plan. Now for the detail

Marbella's new general plan (PGOM) won final approval on 18 September and was published in the Junta's official bulletin on 9 October. It replaces the 1986 plan, sets the town's urban land at about 53 million m², and opens 2.86 million m² of existing urban land to redevelopment. In those areas, 20% of the new floor space must go to affordable or protected housing, and the council takes 10% of the development value. It takes full effect once it appears in the Málaga provincial bulletin, which had not happened by 22 October.

On 22 October more than 100 architects, engineers and officials met to learn how to apply it. The PGOM decides where Marbella can grow; the second plan, the POU, still being drafted, will decide heights, densities and uses plot by plot, and that is what sets land values.

What it means here
After decades of legal limbo, owners get firmer ground. But until the POU is approved, anyone valuing a plot for redevelopment is still partly guessing.

04 · Marbella East · projects

Two luxury resorts, €760 million, one week

Branded homes between them

St. Regis Las Dunas. Unveiled on 21 October in Las Chapas, beside the Real de Zaragoza dunes: a 169-room St. Regis (Marriott) and 151 branded residences of two to five bedrooms, on more than 160,000 m² with over 350 m of beach. The backers are Hong Kong's Platinum Estates and Sierra Blanca Estates. The budget has grown from €350m in 2024 to about €500m, approval took more than eight years, and opening is planned for 2031 with about 450 permanent jobs. Local environmentalists say the dunes will suffer; the developers say they will clean and restore them.

Oakmond. On the same day, ground was broken on the old Incosol site, closed since 2012. Ilanga Capital (Pelayo Cortina Koplowitz) is building a 140-room "longevity" resort with 28 residences for about €260m, with €145m lent by Goldman Sachs Alternatives. Opening is planned for the third quarter of 2029. Add the Don Carlos, reopened this year after a €43m rebuild, and the east side is turning into Marbella's new luxury-hotel corridor.

What it means here
Branded residences usually sell at a premium to the area, and a five-star address nearby tends to lift the resale homes around it. Elviria and Las Chapas have been the quieter, cheaper side of town. That gap may narrow.

05 · On the road · Elviria

The Elviria exit that vanished overnight

Junction 1034 on the A-7, the way into southern Elviria and Golden Beach, was shut in late September with little warning. Acciona closed it, with the state roads authority's permission, as part of its BLU project at Estrella del Mar (58 apartments and 88 townhouses). Drivers are now sent through the exit by the Don Carlos, and residents report 15 to 20-minute detours on unfamiliar streets. Other access points in Las Chapas were closed the same week.

On 30 September the mayor asked Madrid to reopen the junction until the new roads are finished. The PSOE opposition replied that the council itself licensed the closure in July 2025. The old junction was closed for safety, as there was no room to speed up before joining the A-7, but the replacement roads were not ready when it shut.

What it means here
Access is part of a home's price. Buyers viewing in Elviria this autumn will meet the detour, so it is worth knowing the story and the expected fix.

06 · Ports · La Bajadilla

La Bajadilla is unblocked after 15 years

On 22 October the Junta's development minister, Mario Muñoz-Atanet, confirmed that the expansion of Marbella's La Bajadilla port is going ahead, after 15 years of court cases and appeals. A new 40-year concession, won by the local consortium Marina Internacional de Marbella, brings more than €200m of private investment. The plan takes berths from about 268 to roughly 750, for yachts of up to 70 m, with shops, restaurants, a renewed fishing quay and about 1,000 parking spaces.

The new operator takes over on 1 January 2027. Then come about 11 months of detailed design and 36 months of building, so the new marina is realistically a 2030 story. Meanwhile 11 companies are bidding for Estepona's port, and Puerto Banús is finishing a €9m pontoon renewal.

What it means here
A big-yacht marina east of the old town pulls money, restaurants and foot traffic toward the Bajadilla and the Casco Antiguo, areas that have lagged behind Banús.

Valuer Gesvalt's third-quarter figures put Málaga province at €3,149 per m², up 8.9% in a year and 64.5% since 2020. Rents average €17.71 per m² a month, up 4.6%, the highest in Andalucía. Marbella is the dearest town in the province, with a standard 90 m² home at around €360,000 and rent above €2,000 a month. Spain as a whole rose faster, 10.6%, but from a much lower base (€2,085 per m²).

Gesvalt calls it "a certain normalisation": still rising, just less steeply. Sales are thin too. National figures for August were up 3.4% on a year earlier, but January to August is down 2.3%, and a shortage of homes for sale is the usual explanation.

What it means here
Slower growth plus a shortage of stock usually means firm prices and longer negotiations, not falls. The prime end follows its own, international market (next story).

08 · Prime · €10m+

€10 million sales are no longer rare, and you made the papers

idealista ran a story on 26 October about €10m-plus homes becoming routine in Marbella, built around Drumelia: more than 20 sales of €10m or more in five years, worth almost €430m together, and more than 20 deals in La Zagaleta alone worth over €250m. Its point: at this level buyers often decide from abroad, so the preparation has to match the size of the decision.

The money shows up in the tax data too. On 2024 figures from the Agencia Tributaria, Benahavís, home of La Zagaleta, is now Spain's second-richest municipality, with an average gross income of €79,041, up 83% in a year. Only Pozuelo de Alarcón, near Madrid, is higher. Experts warn that in a small town a handful of very high earners can move the average, which is rather the point.

What it means here
A national piece about €10m deals is useful marketing in itself. And Benahavís's ranking is an easy, independent fact to quote to buyers asking who their neighbours would be.

09 · Airport · tourism

Málaga airport heads for a record year

Málaga handled 2.8 million passengers in September, up 6.8%, and 22.1 million from January to September, up 6.6%. That puts 2026 on course for more than 27 million, beating last year's record of 26.7 million. It is Spain's fourth-busiest airport after Madrid, Barcelona and Palma.

Seven in ten international arrivals now fly low-cost (72%), and the UK alone is almost 30% of the total. One crack: Ryanair is dropping its winter Málaga–Brussels route, blaming Belgian ticket taxes, and its boss is warning of big fare rises.

What it means here
Direct, cheap flights are what keep second-home owners coming back several times a year. A route cut matters more to the coast than a headline record.
