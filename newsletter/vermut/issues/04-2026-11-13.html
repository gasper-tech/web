01 · La Bajadilla · the long story

Sixteen years, two bidders and a court case: how La Bajadilla finally got its port

€200m
Private investment, no cost to the Junta
268 → 750
Berths, for boats up to 70 m
40 years
Length of the concession

On 3 November the Junta signed a 40-year concession for Marbella's fishing and leisure port, La Bajadilla, with Marina Internacional de Marbella, which will take over on 1 January. The story starts in 2010. Two groups bid. The winner, with 353 points against 307, was a joint venture tied to the Qatari Al Thani family, and it included Marbella town hall itself. It was awarded the contract in 2011, never started work, ran up more than 52 months of delays and stopped paying its fee. The Junta cancelled it in November 2016 and kept its guarantee.

The runner-up, Marina Internacional, had gone to court arguing a town hall could not sit inside a bidding consortium. The Andalusian high court agreed in 2017, the Supreme Court refused the appeal in 2018, and the contract fell back to the company that came second eight years earlier. Then came eight more years of environmental and coastal permits. The new operator, which includes Marinas del Mediterráneo (Estepona, La Duquesa), will pay €1 million a year and put in €200 million to take the port from 268 to about 750 berths.

What it means here
A port that can take 70 m yachts, a short walk from the old town, changes the east end of the Paseo Marítimo. Works have no start date yet, so frontline flats nearby are a long bet rather than a quick one.

02 · Tax · holiday lets

Holiday-let VAT passes by one vote

Parliament's standing committee confirmed the housing decree (RDL 29/2026) on Thursday by 35 votes to 34, with PSOE, Sumar, PNV, ERC, Bildu and Podemos in favour and PP, Vox and Junts against. Tourist lets of 30 nights or fewer, which were VAT-free, now move to 10% VAT from 1 December. Councils in stressed housing areas can add up to 150% to IBI on some tourist homes, rent rises are capped at 2% until the end of 2027, and first-time buyers get 0% state-backed loans for up to 20% of the price (maximum €50,000).

The second decree, which would have turned rental contracts into rolling five-year renewals, failed 34–35 after the PNV voted against it. PP leader Feijóo has promised to repeal the measures if he wins on 29 November, so the new VAT may not last long.

What it means here
Owners letting a Marbella flat to tourists need to price in VAT for stays from December. Buyers looking at holiday-let yield should model both the 10% case and a possible repeal after the election.

03 · Town hall · follow the money

Marbella's debt rises to €280m as public land goes to private hands

€279.7m
Total council debt forecast, end of 2027
€104m
Bank and state-fund loans in it
€0
Bank debt in 2017, per Marbella24horas

The council's own budget report, seen by Marbella24horas, shows total debt rising from €271.2 million at the end of this year to €279.7 million at the end of 2027, even after repaying €19.7 million. The cause is a new €28.1 million loan from the state's economic stimulus fund. Old debts to the tax office, Social Security and the Junta, mostly left from the Gil years, keep falling; bank-style debt rises to €104 million.

At the same budget presentation, Mayor Ángeles Muñoz defended handing municipal plots to private operators for an annual fee, recently a private hospital and clinic, before that a Swedish school, a padel club and the El Ancón beach club. Her case: an empty plot costs money, a concession brings jobs and a fee, and the cash can go to busier areas. Critics say the same plots could have gone to affordable housing, which the mayor herself said in 2024 was hard to find land for.

What it means here
Frozen IBI is partly paid for with borrowing. And every plot that goes to a hospital, school or club shapes the neighbourhood around it, which is worth checking before buying next to an empty municipal plot.

04 · Estepona · court

Estepona's mayor will stand trial over a 46-day job

A court in Estepona has sent Mayor José María García Urbano to trial at Málaga's provincial court on charges of abuse of office and misuse of public funds. The case is about a woman he hired as a temporary official by decree on 13 February 2023, for 46 days. She was paid about €5,300. Prosecutors say she did no work and that she and the mayor were in a relationship.

Prosecutors want 1 year and 11 months in prison and almost 6 years' ban from public office for the mayor; the PSOE, as private prosecutor, asks for 5½ years. The mayor has repaid the salary and Social Security costs, says no crime was committed, and wants the trial held soon. His defence says HR reports and witnesses show the work was done, and that earlier complaints over the same facts were dismissed. No trial date is set.

What it means here
García Urbano has run Estepona's building boom for years, and the town now has the fastest-rising prices on the coast. A conviction with a ban would change who leads the town hall, but not the planning already approved.

05 · Prime market · buyers

Plenty of buyers, not enough of the right villas

An idealista piece this week, based on DM Properties' autumn report, says Marbella's top end is slower but not short of buyers. Clients take longer, compare more and want finished homes rather than projects. In La Zagaleta, searches for sea-view or fully updated homes sit at €15 to €30 million, and few properties tick every box. Above about €4 million, a gym, spa, sauna and cinema are increasingly expected.

The busiest areas are the Golden Mile, Sierra Blanca, Cascada de Camoján, Nagüeles and Puente Romano, with Nueva Andalucía active across more price points and Guadalmina seeing new interest. Taste is shifting from the white modern box to Andalusian or Mediterranean exteriors with contemporary interiors. US buyers are a growing group, comparing Marbella with high-cost US cities and taxes.

What it means here
Sellers of dated villas in top locations face a choice: renovate to the new standard or price for the buyer who will. Finished, characterful homes with wellness space are the scarce product.

06 · Rail and air · getting here

Still no fast trains to Madrid, but the British keep booking

Two weeks after the Urda crash, the judge had still not allowed (as of Tuesday) the track to be rebuilt, and the ministry says reopening will take up to ten days after permission comes. Ouigo has cancelled Madrid–Andalucía trains until at least 22 November. When trains return, there will be fewer departures but double-length trains, until the new ERTMS signalling is in place, expected early 2027.

The British market looks steadier. At the World Travel Market in London, Jet2 told the Costa del Sol tourist board its 2027 bookings are up 9%, easyJet Holidays reported almost 19% more customers and Thomas Cook 26% more bookings. The board expects about 3.3 million British visitors this year, spending around €4.6 billion.

What it means here
For Madrid clients, flying stays the only quick option into late November. For the British market, strong 2027 bookings support short-term rental demand, whatever the new VAT does.

07 · Fuengirola · housing

Fuengirola pays for 82 affordable rental homes itself

Fuengirola has signed the contract for 82 affordable rental homes in Torreblanca, on two council plots on Calle Oropéndola totalling more than 8,500 m². The joint venture Urbasur-Marvel will design and build them for €11.7 million, paid entirely by the council, with 22 months for the job. The winning bid added 17 homes to the 65 in the original tender.

The mix is 30 one-bedroom, 39 two-bedroom, 12 three-bedroom and one larger home. Rents have not been published, but the homes stay council-owned and rented at affordable rates. It is the opposite model to Marbella's, where the council prefers to give developers tax breaks to build rent-capped homes.

What it means here
Staff housing is the coast's hidden constraint. Every affordable flat near the A-7 helps the hotels, restaurants and villa services that the prime market depends on.

08 · Fuengirola · opening

El Corte Inglés doubles up in Fuengirola

El Corte Inglés reopened its Fuengirola store on 12 November after a big refit. It now has six retail floors instead of three, and more than 22,000 m² of shop space, up from 14,500. Brands include Dior, Jo Malone, Hugo Boss, Polo Ralph Lauren and Tommy Hilfiger, with a new home section and a Club del Gourmet food hall in place of the old Hipercor.

The company expects around 40% of its customers to be foreign, and has added a tax-free desk and interpreters. The store employs about 600 people, one of the biggest employers in Fuengirola.

What it means here
With Banús's own El Corte Inglés and this one, the coast has two full department stores about half an hour apart. Useful for clients furnishing a new home fast.

09 · Gibraltar · update

PP will vote against the Gibraltar treaty in Brussels

The PP has said its MEPs will vote against the EU–UK Gibraltar treaty when the European Parliament votes on 14 December. It argues Gibraltar's status is unchanged, the tax and business rules are not a level playing field, and the treaty was never put to Spain's parliament.

Foreign minister José Manuel Albares accused the PP of trying to "sabotage" the deal. He said a rejection could bring back border queues for about 15,000 daily cross-border workers and hit some 300,000 people in the Campo de Gibraltar. The treaty, which removed hard border checks, has applied provisionally since July. Albares says he is confident of a majority.

What it means here
The western coast, from Sotogrande to Estepona, depends on an easy border. A December vote that goes wrong would be felt first by Gibraltar workers and buyers in San Roque and Manilva.
