01 · Puerto Banús · the long story

92 flats, 244 parking spaces and 3,000 hectares: following the Assad money to Marbella

€77m
Suspicious transactions flagged, 2013–2020
92 + 244
Flats and parking spaces in Benabola
500+
Properties a Spanish court ordered seized in 2020

This week Le Monde published a leak from inside Deutsche Bank. An internal report from 2021 lists 7,944 suspicious transactions worth about €77 million, made between 2013 and 2020 and linked to Rifaat al Assad, uncle of Syria's former president and known as the "butcher of Hama". The money moved through a Gibraltar bank, with Deutsche Bank as correspondent, and through a Spanish company that was a direct client of the bank.

The trail leads to the coast. The family's assets here reportedly include 92 flats and 244 parking spaces in the Benabola complex in Puerto Banús, shops, a finca in El Ancón and more than 3,000 hectares in Benahavís. In 2020 a Spanish court ordered more than 500 properties seized, with alleged laundering put at over €600 million. Rifaat al Assad died in January without being tried. Since May 2025 the case has sat with the Marbella courts and targets eight relatives. No hearing date has been set. Deutsche Bank says it settled the matter with a €7 million fine in 2022.

What it means here
Seized property can sit frozen for years, which matters if you are selling next door or managing a community with frozen units. It is also a reminder that anti-money-laundering checks on buyers are not paperwork: agents are part of the chain, and banks are now being judged on what they let through.

02 · Regional plan · planning

After twenty years, the western Costa del Sol gets a regional plan

9
Towns covered, Fuengirola to Manilva
6
New AP-7 access points proposed
13
Priority growth areas

On Wednesday the Junta approved the regional land plan (POT) for Fuengirola, Mijas, Marbella, Estepona, Casares, Manilva, Ojén, Istán and Benahavís. It replaces the 2006 plan the Supreme Court annulled, and the regional housing minister said it had been stuck for about twenty years. It takes effect the day after it appears in the BOJA.

It sets out six new AP-7 access points (Casares, Benahavís, Istán, near Hospital Costa del Sol, Cala de Mijas and Las Lagunas), a bus corridor on the A-7, the coastal train extended from Fuengirola, new parks at Guadaiza and Elviria, and 13 priority growth areas, mostly along the A-7. It also states the aim of removing the AP-7 toll, at least for residents, but the toll, the accesses and the train all need the central government to agree and pay. The plan overrides town plans, which get four years to adapt.

What it means here
This is the map the next decade of growth will follow. A new AP-7 access near Benahavís or Istán would change journey times for whole valleys, but treat the rail and toll ideas as wishes until there is money behind them.

03 · Holiday lets · tax

From Tuesday, holiday lets charge 10% VAT. Here is what owners have to do

10%
VAT on stays of up to 30 nights
1 Dec
Start date

From 1 December, furnished homes let to the same guest for up to 30 nights carry 10% VAT, and private owners count as businesses for this purpose. Owners need to register with Hacienda (form 036), issue proper invoices, keep VAT books and file a quarterly return (form 303).

There is an upside. VAT paid on costs linked to the let, such as utilities, repairs, furniture, advice and advertising, can be deducted with proper invoices. On a €1,000 stay, keeping the price the same leaves €909 for the owner and €91 for Hacienda; adding VAT on top makes it €1,100. Hacienda has not yet said how to treat bookings made before 1 December for stays after it. Sunday's election does not change the rule by itself.

What it means here
Owners who let a Marbella flat through Airbnb or an agency need a gestor now, not in January. For buyers, gross yield figures quoted before this month are out of date: run the numbers again with VAT.

04 · Town plan · planning

The new town plan could mean 3,000 protected homes and legal status for Gil-era houses

20%
Share of new growth areas reserved for public housing
~18,000
Gil-era homes the plan can regularise

Two weeks after the new general plan took effect, the mayor met the Andalusian developers' federation, FADECO, at its assembly in Marbella. The council says the growth areas carry a 20% legal reserve for public housing, enough for more than 3,000 protected homes. The federation says about 50 projects are already in process.

The plan also opens the way to regularising about 18,000 of the roughly 30,000 homes built irregularly under Jesús Gil. On Tuesday the town finally wound up the last of the Gil-era municipal companies, twenty years on. Agents are still asking for faster licences and one clear reading of the rules.

What it means here
Regularisation is the quiet big story for resale: thousands of homes with planning doubts could become easier to sell and mortgage. Ask which category a property falls into before pricing it.

05 · Shops · planning

More room for shops, and a door open to big stores

A change to Marbella's planning rules for commercial premises, first reported by SUR on 8 November, is going through the town hall. Shops would be able to use their first basement for the business without it counting towards buildable floor area, where today it counts at 50%. Mezzanines could cover 75% of the floor below instead of 60%, also without counting.

A new commercial sub-zone would let large stores open in any commercial-zoned area, as long as the developer justifies traffic, height and impact on the street. It was drafted as an amendment to the rules of the old 1986 plan, so it is not yet clear how it carries over now that the new plan is in force. No date has been given.

What it means here
Commercial units with basements and high ceilings could become more useful, and so more valuable. Worth flagging to owners of retail space before the change is approved.

06 · Airport · numbers

Málaga airport heads for another record, and Poland is the fastest riser

2.7m

24.8m
January to October, +6.4%
+30%
Growth from Poland

Málaga airport handled almost 2.7 million passengers in October, about 87,000 a day and 4.7% more than a year ago. International traffic grew 6.2%. Over the first ten months it carried 24.8 million.

The UK is still far ahead with 617,000 passengers, followed by Germany, the Netherlands, Italy, France, Ireland, Belgium and Poland. The fastest growth came from Poland (+30%), Bulgaria, Latvia, Hungary, Lithuania and Denmark, all up by about a fifth or more.

What it means here
Visitors today are often buyers tomorrow. Central and Eastern European and Nordic markets are growing fastest; a Polish or Danish version of a listing may be worth more than it was.

07 · Election · spain

Spain votes on Sunday. Housing is on the ballot

The general election is on Sunday 29 November, with Saturday the day of reflection. Feijóo has pledged to undo the holiday-let VAT that starts on Tuesday, so the result will decide whether it lasts.

The debate reached Marbella's own plenary this week. PP and Vox voted down a PSOE motion backing the central government's housing measures, and also rejected a 95% IBI discount for small shops. The new Cortes will meet on 23 December, so any change of course would come in 2027.

What it means here
Clients will ask on Monday what the result means for them. The honest short answer: nothing changes overnight, and tax changes need a new government and a new law.

08 · Town marina · town hall

The marina's 50-year extension earns the mayor a second electoral warning

The provincial electoral board has ruled, for the second time in about ten days, that Marbella's mayor broke electoral law. After a PSOE complaint, it found that a council press release of 5 November about extending the town marina's concession by 50 years, presented as more than €16 million of investment and featuring the mayor, was campaign publicity.

The board ordered the item withdrawn and told the mayor not to repeat it, but imposed no fine because she had already been warned once. The concession itself is not affected.

What it means here
The marina extension still stands and is the bigger news for the Paseo Marítimo: a 50-year operator with money to spend usually lifts frontline values nearby.
