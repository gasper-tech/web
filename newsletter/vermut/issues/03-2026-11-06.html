01 · Tax · holiday lets

Next Thursday decides the 10% holiday-let VAT

The housing decree that brings in 10% VAT on tourist flats (Real Decreto-ley 29/2026) has been in force since 8 October, but the VAT only starts on 1 December if parliament confirms the decree. With the Cortes dissolved for the 29 November election, the vote falls to the Diputación Permanente, the small standing committee, on Thursday 12 November. The legal deadline is the 13th. The same package lets councils add up to 150% to IBI in stressed housing areas and tightens the rules on seasonal lets.

Reports say the government has the votes for at least one of the two decrees, with PSOE, Sumar, ERC, Bildu, PNV and Podemos behind it. If the decree is not confirmed, it lapses, and holiday lets stay VAT-free from December. The vote comes the day after Wednesday's general strike over housing.

What it means here
For owners who let a second home to tourists, a 10% charge on stays of 30 nights or fewer changes the yield maths from December. Buyers looking at a flat for holiday income should wait for Thursday's result before running numbers.

02 · Buyers · data

Foreigners buy a record share of Spain's homes

The Land Registrars' figures for July to September, out on 5 November, show foreigners bought 16.05% of all homes sold in Spain, a record, or just over 26,300 homes. Total sales fell 5.9% on a year ago to 164,148, the third quarter in a row of decline, while foreign purchases rose 11.2%.

In Málaga province, 38.8% of buyers were foreign, second only to Alicante at 46.5%. The British are still the largest group, followed by Moroccans, Dutch and Germans. North Americans paid the most, about €4,469 per m², against €3,221 for EU buyers. The registry's repeat-sales index hit a new high, up 15.8% in a year.

What it means here
Local buyers are being squeezed out by prices and mortgages, and foreign money is filling the gap. In Málaga province, more than a third of buyers are now from abroad.

Idealista's October report puts Marbella's average asking price at €5,974 per m², up just 0.1% on September and 3.1% on a year ago. That is far slower than the national registry index above.

The split by area is the story. Nagüeles and the Golden Mile, the dearest zone at €8,245, fell 1% in the month and are up only 1% in a year. The fastest risers are the cheaper areas: Las Chapas–El Rosario up 7.9% in a year, San Pedro up 5.7% and Marbella town up 5.2%. Río Real–Los Monteros is the only zone down on the year, at −1.7%.

What it means here
Asking prices at the very top have stopped climbing, while demand moves east and west to areas with more room to grow. It is a useful line for sellers on the Golden Mile who still expect last year's increases.

04 · Marbella · budget and taxes

A €477m budget, frozen taxes and a break for rent-capped homes

Marbella presented its 2027 budget on 5 November: €476.9 million, up 5.8% on this year and 47% since 2023. It includes about €68 million of council investment, €6 million for public housing, €7.4 million for car parks (Plaza de Toros, Nueva Andalucía, an underground one in San Pedro and Albergue África), €6.6 million for an athletics stadium and €6.5 million to keep buses free. The plenary approves it this month, and it should take effect on 1 January.

The tax changes approved last Friday go with it. The basic IBI rate stays at 0.65%. Developers who build homes for capped rents get 75% to 95% off the 4% construction tax (ICIO), and those homes keep a 50% IBI discount. The highest IBI rate on leisure and hotel properties drops from 1% to 0.82%, which the council says leaves about €1.1 million with owners.

What it means here
Frozen IBI is good news for owners. The ICIO break is aimed at build-to-rent developers, which could finally bring some rental supply for the staff the town's villas and hotels depend on.

05 · La Zagaleta · seller warning

A €6m villa, a €2m deposit, and almost all of it fake

Police have arrested three people who tried to scam the Austrian owner of a villa in La Zagaleta valued at about €6 million. Posing as advisers with a buyer, they met him in Marbella hotels and restaurants from September and agreed a first payment of €2 million. At the same time, a relative of his was to hand over €300,000 in gold bars as a "commission" at a café in Vienna.

Two men were arrested with the owner at a Golden Mile hotel and a third in Vienna. Officers seized €2,093,800 in cash, of which only about €30,000 was real money. This is the classic "rip deal": fake buyers build trust over long lunches, then swap counterfeit cash for real money, gold or jewellery. The owner grew suspicious of the rush and the odd payment terms, and checked with his own country's police.

What it means here
Prime sellers are the target. Any buyer who wants to pay in cash, rushes the deal or asks the seller for a deposit or commission up front is a red flag. Real purchases go through a notary and a bank.

06 · Rail · update

The minister quits, the fast trains still don't run

Transport minister Óscar Puente resigned on Tuesday night, a week after the Urda crash that killed at least 69 people, and dropped out of the PSOE's election lists. Economy minister Carlos Cuerpo is running Transport for now. Before leaving, Puente said the Madrid–Andalucía high-speed line could reopen within ten days of a judge allowing repairs, at first with fewer trains, perhaps one every 15 minutes instead of every 5 to 10 on Madrid–Sevilla.

Some press reports point to 14 November, but nothing is confirmed, and Renfe still isn't selling tickets before 13 December. At the World Travel Market in London on Tuesday, Francisco Salado, who heads the Costa del Sol tourist board, asked Madrid for an emergency transport plan. UK airline seats to the coast this winter are already 5% down on last year, at about 1.3 million. Ryanair is adding 50,000 seats and Volotea more flights between Madrid and Andalucía.

What it means here
Madrid clients still need to fly. Iberia's capped fares (€99 economy, €149 business) end on Sunday 8 November, so flights may get dearer next week.

07 · Commuter trains · infrastructure

A bad week on the Fuengirola train

The C1 commuter line from Málaga to Fuengirola, via the airport, Torremolinos and Benalmádena, had a rough week. The last train to Fuengirola broke down on Sunday night, services were suspended after a fatal accident near Torreblanca on Monday, and on Thursday a faulty train caused 37 service alerts in one day, with waits of up to an hour at Plaza Mayor.

The line carried most of the 15.5 million Cercanías passengers in Málaga last year, and some stretches run at 150% of seated capacity. Seven new high-capacity trains (€66.7 million) are not due until 2028. Adif is also doubling 2.8 km of track between the airport and Campamento Benítez.

What it means here
The train still stops at Fuengirola, with no line yet to Marbella. For staff and clients who rely on it to reach the airport, allow extra time until the new trains arrive.

08 · Port · project

Marbella wants the town marina for 50 years

The council is working with the Junta de Andalucía to extend its concession on the Puerto Deportivo Virgen del Carmen, the marina in front of the old town, to 50 years instead of the 25 first planned. Mayor Ángeles Muñoz met the regional transport minister on 4 November and the port's director on the 5th.

The council plans to invest more than €16 million in modernising the port. It has told current businesses that rents and community charges stay exactly as they are while the paperwork goes through, and that they will have priority to stay on under the longer concession. No timetable or list of works has been published yet.

What it means here
A longer concession is what makes a big refit pay. A smarter marina next to the Paseo Marítimo helps the frontline flats and the town centre.

09 · Markets · opening

San Pedro gets a temporary market, Las Chapas a new one next

San Pedro Alcántara's market is about to be rebuilt from the ground up. To keep traders open, the council has installed temporary modules next to the current site, with a fishmonger, butcher, florist, greengrocer, frozen food and a café. Cold rooms, toilets and a central pergola are still to come, and the move should happen before the end of the year.

When the new San Pedro market is finished, the modules will move to Las Chapas, to a council plot opposite the town-hall office and the church. It is meant to be a food and shopping draw, with operators chosen by tender and site works planned for next year.

What it means here
Las Chapas has very little in the way of a centre. A market there adds the kind of everyday amenity that makes the east side work for families who live there all year.
