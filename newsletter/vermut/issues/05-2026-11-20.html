01 · San Pedro tunnel · the long story

Valued at €2.6m in 2003, now a €39m bill: the land under the San Pedro tunnel

€2.6m
What the valuation board said the land was worth
€27.6m
What the court says it was worth, plus 5%
~€39m
Total with occupation pay and interest

In 2003, under mayor Julián Muñoz, Marbella town hall expropriated three plots in San Pedro Alcántara, about 8,400 m² in all, for the road works that became the San Pedro tunnel and boulevard. Two of the plots are now part of Avenida de Lyon and the third is a stretch of Avenida del Mediterráneo. The Junta's valuation board put the land at €2.6 million. The owners never accepted that figure and have fought it in court ever since.

This month the Andalusian high court (TSJA) sided with them. It set the value at €27.6 million plus a 5% supplement, and with pay for years of temporary occupation and legal interest the bill comes to almost €39 million. The tunnel itself opened in 2012 and cost more than €85 million. The town hall can still appeal to the Supreme Court.

What it means here
This is how land valuations really work in Spain: the first official number is a starting offer, and interest runs for as long as the case does. For owners facing an expropriation or a road plan, it pays to contest the price. For the town, it is a bill close to a tenth of a year's budget.

02 · Town plan · planning

Marbella's new general plan is in force. The detailed rules come next

40m → 53m m²
Urban land, 1986 plan vs the new one
2028
Target for final approval of the detailed plan

The new general plan (PGOM) took effect last Saturday, ending decades of working under the 1986 plan and the annulled one from 2010. Urban land grows from about 40 to about 53 million m². This week the mayor, Ángeles Muñoz, met the estate agents' association LPA to explain what comes next.

The PGOM sets the big picture. The rules that matter most for a single plot, uses and buildability, sit in a separate detailed plan (POU), with a first draft due for public display between late 2026 and early 2027. Initial approval is planned for 2027 and final approval for 2028. LPA, which has more than 100 member firms, warned that land for new homes is still short and asked for faster licences and one consistent reading of the plan.

What it means here
Buyers will ask whether the new plan changes their plot. The honest answer, for now, is that the POU will decide most of it. Watch for the public display period: that is when owners and agents can see and object to the details.

03 · 2027 budget · town hall

A €477m budget passes its first vote, with only the PP behind it

€476.9m
Total, up 5.8% on this year
€85m
Room for investment

Tuesday's plenary gave initial approval to the 2027 budget. The PP voted for it, Vox abstained, and PSOE and Opción Sampedreña voted against, calling it electoral and inflated. The total is 47% higher than the 2023 accounts. Staff costs alone pass €200 million.

For property, the lines to note are €6 million for public housing and bonuses of up to 95% on taxes for some limited-rent developments. The tax breaks on IBI, IAE, ICIO and car tax all stay. Works include €7.4m for a public car park and sports facilities at Albergue África, €4m for asphalt, €1.8m for the San Pedro bus interchange and €1m to start dualling the Ronda road. It should take effect on 1 January.

What it means here
No rise in property taxes for 2027, and the ICIO break on building works stays. The Ronda road money is only a start, so don't sell a villa on a faster commute yet.

04 · AVE · transport

The judge lets Adif repair the line. Tickets go on sale for 13 December

On Thursday the judge investigating the Urda crash allowed Adif to start repairing the track, on condition that the damage and the repairs are fully documented. That was the last block on work. Unofficial reports talk of trains back in about ten days, around 29 November.

Renfe is not selling Málaga–Madrid tickets for travel before 13 December, and the new transport minister has not given a date. When trains return there will be fewer of them at first, with some running as double units.

What it means here
Clients flying in for December viewings can plan on the train from mid-month, not before. Keep a flight as the safe option until a date is official.

05 · Estepona · taxes

Estepona keeps half off IBI for residents in 2027

50%
IBI cut for registered residents
€7m+
Set aside for tax bonuses and exemptions

Estepona will keep its 50% cut on IBI, the yearly property tax, for owners registered on the padrón, applied automatically on the bill. Retired and long-term unemployed residents stay exempt from the refuse fee, about 2,000 bills.

There is also an extra 3% off IBI, refuse fee, IAE and car tax for anyone who pays by direct debit. The bylaws went to the town's plenary on Thursday for approval.

What it means here
For a buyer choosing between Estepona and its neighbours, registering on the padrón halves a running cost. It is a simple line to put in a cost comparison.

06 · Jobs · economy

Marbella sheds nearly 3,800 jobs after a strong summer

Marbella lost almost 3,800 jobs between September and October, a fall of about 3.3%, as the summer season wound down. A drop at this time of year is normal on the coast, where hotels, beach clubs and restaurants cut staff.

The size of the autumn fall is the clearest sign of how much the local economy still leans on the summer. This week the business club CIT Marbella also signed a tourism training and research deal with the University of Málaga.

What it means here
A seasonal economy shapes the rental market: summer lets earn most of the year's income. Buyers looking at year-round rentals should check winter demand, not only August prices.

07 · Election · spain

Sánchez and Feijóo face off on TV, nine days before the vote

The two main candidates met in a televised debate this week ahead of the general election on Sunday 29 November. It was the main set piece of a short campaign.

For property, the vote matters directly. Feijóo has pledged to repeal the 10% VAT on holiday lets that starts on 1 December, which passed by a single vote. The result will also decide who writes the next housing law and whether tighter rules on short-term rentals go further.

What it means here
Owners with holiday lets will start charging VAT on 1 December whatever happens. If the government changes, it could be undone in 2027, so it is worth keeping clients informed rather than rushing decisions.

08 · Fuengirola · projects

Fuengirola's €14m market misses another opening date

€14m
Cost of the new market
H1 2027
New expected opening

Work on Fuengirola's new Mercacentro started in January 2024 on an 18-month plan, so it was due to open in July 2025. A revised date of 1 October 2026 has also passed. It is now expected in the first half of 2027.

The causes given are a weak basement slab, problems getting materials and trouble with the building consortium. When it opens it will have more than 11,000 m², 39 stalls, restaurants, a business incubator, a public terrace and underground parking, in a cross-laminated timber structure.

What it means here
Public projects on the coast often run a year or two late. When a listing leans on a nearby project, check how far along the works really are.

09 · Marina · coast

Divers pull 500 kg of rubbish from under the town marina

Divers working under Marbella's Virgen del Carmen marina, in the centre of town, brought up about 500 kg of rubbish, including phones and a power tool.

This is the town marina whose 50-year concession made news earlier this month, and the haul shows how much upkeep the waterfront needs. Separately, Marbella's water treatment plant and Málaga's reservoirs were added to a new maintenance plan this week.

What it means here
A clean front is part of what buyers pay for on the Paseo Marítimo. Waterfront upkeep, and who pays for it under a concession, is a fair question to ask before buying frontline.
